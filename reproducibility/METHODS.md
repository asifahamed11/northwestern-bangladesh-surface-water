# Implemented research methods and interpretation limits

## Sources, domains and units

The verified union comprises Naogaon, Nawabganj (reported as Chapainawabganj), and Rajshahi. The union is descriptive and overlaps all districts; it is not a fourth independent replicate. Native JRC, CHIRPS and ERA grids are retained. JRC pixel area and climate polygon intersections are evaluated in equal-area EPSG:6933. Raster pixel-center district assignment is distinct from exact polygon climate-cell weighting; their areas need not equal exactly.

JRC annual classes: 0 no observations, 1 nonwater, 2 seasonal, 3 permanent. Monthly: 0 no observations, 1 nonwater, 2 water. Masked exported values are nodata -9999, not valid zero-water observations. The provider documents Landsat Collection 1 versus Collection 2 location differences; identical export grid dimensions do not prove co-registration. Primary long-term estimates stop in 2021; 2000–2024 results with a 2022 step are labeled sensitivity. The correction of 2016–2021 annual assets is retained. New monthly 2022–2024 selections use genuine provider indexes/year/month, because those assets have no `system:time_start`. [JRC download/version information](https://global-surface-water.appspot.com/download), [annual class definition](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/JRC_GSW1_4_YearlyHistory).

CHIRPS V3 monthly rainfall is the sum of six actual pentads, requiring all six valid per pixel. Annual rainfall sums all twelve monthly values. Regional means use exact cell-intersection areas and finite-data support. Rainfall is mm. ERA monthly accumulated precipitation and potential evaporation are converted m to mm; Kelvin becomes Celsius. Annual temperature is weighted by actual calendar-month day counts. Wind components are monthly means; their vector magnitude is not mean instantaneous wind speed. [CHIRPS catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/UCSB-CHC_CHIRPS_V3_PENTAD), [ERA5-Land catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/ECMWF_ERA5_LAND_MONTHLY_AGGR).

ERA-Land potential evaporation is a model evaporation-demand proxy with the original signed flux retained, and sign-reversed demand separately named. It is not FAO reference ET0. ECMWF distinguishes ERA-Land open-water/pan treatment from ERA5 agricultural potential evaporation; use the producer definition when the catalog wording differs. This pipeline computes precipitation-only SPI, **not validated SPEI**. [ECMWF ERA5-Land documentation](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/spaces/CKB/pages/140385202/ERA5-Land+data+documentation).

## Observation quality and fixed spatial cohorts

The retrospective QC reference footprint is annual seasonal/permanent water in at least one 2000–2024 year. It excludes some monthly-only transient water and does not represent an independent prior wetland inventory. Primary candidate months must have at least 95% observation coverage on both the whole raster district domain and this fixed footprint. Thresholds 80 and 90 are reported as sensitivity. The primary 95 choice was made from coverage/common-support retention diagnostics, before examining estimated effects; it is a study design choice, not a recommended universal cutoff.

For each threshold/region, the common monthly cohort is the intersection of valid pixels across every candidate month. Only months in the candidate set with **100% cohort observation** receive a water fraction. Requirements: at least 48 candidate months and at least 20% retention of the relevant reference footprint. Annual comparisons use one fixed annual-valid cohort across all 25 source years. These cohorts support restricted spatial comparisons, not district-wide extrapolation. Dropped cloudy pixels/months can induce nonrandom selection; this is not corrected by meeting a coverage threshold. Inspect calendar coverage and sensitivity before generalizing to wet seasons.

Additional frozen hydroperiod cohorts use a strict majority of the first five annual source classes (2000–2004), with all five years observed: at least 3 seasonal years or at least 3 permanent years. Ambiguous or nonwater initial classes are excluded from those two groups. Group membership does not change later. Class-cohort monthly outcomes/recovery begin in 2005; all-water outcomes begin in 2000. A fraction for baseline permanent water means fraction of that frozen cohort observed as monthly water; it does not mean permanent area newly reclassified each month.

## Trend and climate models

Annual unadjusted class/total areas are fitted against year; full-period sensitivity includes a 2022 version step. Theil-Sen slopes are descriptive. Primary district trend tests receive Benjamini-Yekutieli false-discovery adjustment within the reported trend family. Separate annual permanent/seasonal climate models use logit class shares, year, annual rainfall, day-weighted temperature and annual evaporation demand; full-period sensitivity adds the version step. Separate annual adjusted-class shares must not be summed into a compositional total. Their time coefficient is logit share per year, not km²/year. Primary district annual climate time coefficients receive their own BY adjustment family.

Monthly models separately fit all-water and the two initial-class cohorts. Predictors: years since January 2000, one annual sine/cosine harmonic, three-calendar-month rainfall sum, monthly temperature, and monthly evaporation demand. Fractions are numerically clipped to [1e-6, 1-1e-6] only for logit fitting. Original observed fractions are unchanged. Weather effects and trends are associations; irrigation, pumping, river management, land use and spatially varying water source mechanisms are not causally identified. Predictor collinearity/rank checks reject unusable models; modest passing condition numbers do not prove correct model specification.

Fixed-climate expectations hold weather at each region's 2000–2021 calendar-month median (annual models: median annual weather). Adjusted observed fraction is `expit(logit(observed fraction) - (fitted current-climate logit - fitted reference-climate logit))`. Outputs explicitly state `model_derived`; they are not missing-data replacement. Conditional coefficient intervals use Student-t critical values with residual degrees of freedom and finite-sample corrected Bartlett HAC covariance. Calendar-distance lag matching uses actual months/years, including gaps, rather than adjacent retained rows. These intervals remain approximate; no causal or exact small-sample guarantee is made. [Statsmodels HAC reference](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.regression.linear_model.RegressionResults.get_robustcov_results.html).

Validation folds use calendar test starts 2009, 2013, 2017, 2020, two test years, and training strictly before the test period. Monthly training excludes the preceding three calendar months; annual folds use all earlier years and require twelve training years/two test years. All feature scaling/fitting is training-only. Comparators are training-only calendar-month means (monthly) or annual mean class fraction (annual). RMSE/MAE describe predictive fit for the accepted cohort/calendar, not independently validated water classification. Cohorts are retrospectively selected using observation availability; these folds are conditional hindcast checks, not operational forecast deployment validation. [Calendar spacing concern in standard time-series CV](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.TimeSeriesSplit.html).

Monthly time coefficient p-values are exploratory and unadjusted; do not declare a family-wide finding from individual nominal p-values. Check annual adjusted families, sensitivity, predictive errors and reference evidence. The basic model does not include nonlinear distributed lags, causal instruments, multilevel dependence or spatial-autocorrelation corrections across districts.

## SPI and recovery

SPI-3/SPI-6 uses complete-calendar rolling rainfall accumulations and calendar-month-specific gamma maximum likelihood, with location fixed at zero, a separately estimated zero probability, and normal quantile transformation. Calibration is fixed 1981–2020; at least 30 usable accumulations and 10 positive accumulations are required per calendar month. Probabilities are bounded numerically at 1e-8 and 1-1e-8. Failed/insufficient calibration generates blank SPI for affected months, not an alternative short-baseline index. Early accumulated windows naturally lack preceding data. [SPI calibration guidance](https://climatedataguide.ucar.edu/climate-data/standardized-precipitation-index-spi), [SciPy gamma fitting](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.gamma.html).

An SPI-3 episode begins at <= -1 and ends at the first >= 0 month; missing climate values censor an active event. Water seasonality is removed with each cohort's pre-2022 calendar-month observed mean, requiring at least five observations for that calendar month. These are observed-water anomalies, not climate-adjusted modeled recovery outcomes. The pre-event baseline is the mean of the preceding three consecutive observed anomalies. The complete drought phase and climate-end month must be observed within one source collection. Impact is baseline minus the minimum drought-phase anomaly; <=0.02 is reported as no detectable water deficit under the configured threshold.

Recovery is the first month at/after climatic drought end whose anomaly returns to within 0.02 fraction points of the observed pre-event mean and persists for two consecutive observed months. Lag can be zero at the end month. Maximum follow-up is 24 months after climatic end. Missing water, collection boundaries, left onset censoring, follow-up endpoints and confirmation failure censor the event; censored duration is blank. No interpolation bridges a missing month. The 0.02 criterion, reference period and windows are operational choices requiring methodological review. Default implementation does not produce a Kaplan-Meier median from events with informative observational censoring. It reports event-level evidence and censor reasons; confirmed-event durations alone must not be presented as population recovery speed.

## Independent evidence and design-weighted accuracy

Four default target months: March 2018, November 2020, March 2023, November 2024. Within each district/month, 30 observed JRC pixels are sampled without replacement in each predicted binary class, with fixed random seed. IDs are opaque; blinded exports omit predicted class. Dated Sentinel-2 harmonized SR and Sentinel-1 GRD images provide independent interpretation evidence. S2 SCL cloud/shadow/snow/saturated/no-data classes are excluded, but residual haze/shadow can remain. Monthly medians are visual aids, not observed truth at every date. S1 GEE backscatter is already dB. Full pixel footprint and temporal evidence must be inspected, not only a center-point index. [Sentinel-2 catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/COPERNICUS_S2_SR_HARMONIZED), [Sentinel-1 catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/COPERNICUS_S1_GRD).

Verified independent 0/1 labels require true target-month dates and source provenance. JRC/GSW self-reference is rejected. Every planned population stratum must meet minimum verified sample requirements before pooled accuracy. Estimated confusion weights are `population pixels / verified samples × sampled pixel area`; pooled accuracy represents sampled months' observed district pixel area-time, not the accuracy of all historical years. Producer/user water accuracy is computed from the area-weighted confusion matrix. Confidence limits resample within strata with 1000 replicates. The bootstrap is approximate, without spatial clusters or a finite-population correction; verified-subset weights assume interpretable samples are sufficiently representative. Nonrandom unverifiable labels can bias accuracy and require review/extra evidence. Completeness checks alone do not establish that assumption. These references do not establish annual permanent/seasonal classification accuracy or solve Landsat collection registration.

## Reproducibility and readiness

Raw files are hash-checked against the verified preprocessing manifest. Each run records config, package versions, code and prepared/external input hashes, stage status, native masks, model counts, and runtime errors. Additional hashes are rechecked at completion. Protected input/code folders cannot be used as output destinations and existing run directories cannot be overwritten. No simulated data is accepted as a substitute for missing calibration/reference inputs.

`software_run_complete=true` means execution completed with explicit status records. `scientific_publication_ready` stays false: the runner does not automatically approve methodological assumptions, censoring representativeness, independent annual class validation, or manuscript conclusions. GEE scripts require an authenticated Earth Engine account and must be audited/run there; local syntax checks are not evidence of successful remote exports.
