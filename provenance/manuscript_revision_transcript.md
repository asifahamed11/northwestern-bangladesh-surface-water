## Abstract

Changes in satellite-mapped water can inform climate-resilient management only when observation support is explicit. We assessed annual seasonal and permanent water source classes in Naogaon, Chapainawabganj and Rajshahi, northwestern Bangladesh, using JRC histories and gridded climate data. The primary 2000–2021 analysis used a retrospectively selected, annually observed footprint of 1,276.41 km². Exploratory reanalysis compared seven footprint/validity cohorts, multi-year windows, simpler climate models, approximate uncertainty estimates and chronological hindcasts. Seasonal source-class area fell from 938.53 to 699.91 km² between the endpoints (25.42%); comparison of the first and final five-year means gave a smaller decline of 14.70%. A footprint based on water observed in 2000–2004 and annual validity through 2021 gave an 18.79% five-year decline. Negative seasonal coefficients persisted across the tested models, although interval and district support varied. Permanent-class gains were sensitive to observation and processing controls. Static river and 2020 cropland context separated contrasting mapped trajectories without identifying their causes. During 2015–2024, raising the Dynamic World threshold from 0.5 to 0.7 increased conditional radar–optical agreement from 89.04% to 98.71%, while mean paired support fell from 11.07% to 3.29%; wet-season support was particularly sparse. Assisted interpretation of 720 sampled pixel-months assigned 76.54% of design-weighted area-time, with 98.56% conditional agreement but no independent accuracy estimate. No actual drought-recovery duration was identified under nine support/reference specifications. Synthetic pulse diagnostics retaining real gaps and envelope widths also failed to confirm the tested deficits. The results identify a restricted-footprint seasonal decline and the observation requirements for investigating it; district-wide water storage, causal abstraction effects and independently validated resilience remain unmeasured.

Keywords: seasonal surface water; climate adjustment; observation support; drought monitoring; remote sensing uncertainty; Barind Tract; Bangladesh

## 1 Introduction

Seasonal surface water connects rainfall variability with the timing and location of usable water. For water managers, a contraction in mapped seasonal water can indicate a place requiring investigation, but its interpretation depends on what the satellite actually observed. A water map summarizes a classification process, a spatial footprint and a set of acquisition dates. Changes in any of these can affect the apparent hydrological signal. The Landsat-based Global Surface Water record provides a consistent framework for examining long-term water occurrence and annual source classes (Pekel et al., 2016). Its application to local water-management questions nevertheless requires attention to missing observations, class definitions and updates to the underlying archive.

These concerns are relevant to northwestern Bangladesh. The Barind region has pronounced seasonal water stress and substantial dependence on groundwater-supported agriculture. Recent station-based research documented spatially heterogeneous rainfall trends across Naogaon, Chapainawabganj and Rajshahi during 2000–2024 (Esha et al., 2026). Work in the high Barind has also examined heatwaves, rainfall, vegetation, soil moisture and groundwater jointly (Sarker et al., 2026). These studies motivate a surface-water assessment that retains local differences and distinguishes measured water classifications from possible climatic and management mechanisms. Administrative districts provide reporting units for such an assessment, although their boundaries do not define hydrological catchments.

Existing evidence also cautions against assuming one direction of surface-water change throughout Bangladesh. In the southwestern Bengal delta, a Landsat time-series study examined strong seasonality and reported expansion in mapped surface water alongside changing local water management (Huda et al., 2021). Those findings concern another region and observation design. They cannot establish the trajectory of seasonal water in the northwest, but they show why the period, water class and spatial domain of a trend need to be explicit. At the much larger Indus–Ganges–Brahmaputra scale, hydrological modeling has separated the components contributing to total water-storage change (Prusevich et al., 2025). Surface-water class area alone does not provide that decomposition.

Combining sensors offers additional evidence, but agreement between products is conditional on where both can assign labels. Dynamic World provides Sentinel-2-derived land-cover probabilities at 10 m (Brown et al., 2022). Radar can supply observations under cloud conditions that restrict optical mapping, while remaining sensitive to vegetation, surface roughness and the water-detection method. A multi-site comparison of Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 explicitly separated open and vegetated water and evaluated both against higher-resolution imagery (Vanderhoof et al., 2023). In Senegal, field, satellite and modeled pond observations revealed both the value and the limitations of remotely sensed water availability (Slinski et al., 2025). These examples support reporting complementary evidence without treating another satellite product as error-free truth. Research on index-based flood insurance in Bangladesh further shows that data choice can affect the timing and uncertainty of water-related decisions (Saunders et al., 2025).

Drought resilience introduces a stricter temporal requirement. A rainfall deficit can propagate through water stores with delays and persistence, so the end of meteorological drought need not coincide with hydrological recovery (Van Loon, 2015). Estimating a recovery duration requires observations before, during and after the event. A complete collection of monthly files can still lack the continuous, interpretable pixel observations needed for this calculation. Similarly, a large number of human image interpretations does not establish independent accuracy when the reviewed samples were selected by a model or the reviewer saw model suggestions. Sampling design, reference quality and analysis must be considered together (Olofsson et al., 2014; Stehman and Foody, 2019).

We therefore ask three related questions: how did seasonal and permanent source-class areas change within a fixed observed footprint; which annual associations persist after climate, observation-effort and processing-boundary controls; and how much spatial and temporal evidence remains available for sensor comparison and drought-recovery assessment? The contribution is an integrated regional assessment with explicit limits on its estimands, rather than a new classification algorithm. We retain null results, unsupported months and unresolved interpretations throughout. This provides a practical basis for deciding which mapped changes merit local investigation and which resilience claims require additional observations.

## 2 Materials and methods

### 2.1 Study area and analytical design

The study covers Naogaon, Chapainawabganj and Rajshahi in northwestern Bangladesh (Figure 1). The reporting boundary was obtained from geoBoundaries Bangladesh ADM2, boundary identifier BGD-ADM2-16705992, representing 2020 and citing Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics and OCHA ROAP as source organizations. The archived source names Chapainawabganj as Nawabganj; we harmonized its reporting name while preserving the geometry and source identifiers. Boundary metadata, licensing and download identifiers are retained in the reproducibility materials. District estimates and their union are reported separately; the union overlaps all three districts and is not an independent replicate.

The design has three linked but distinct components. The primary component describes annual Joint Research Centre (JRC) source classes during 2000–2021 within a fixed annual-observation footprint. A 2000–2024 extension assesses source-version sensitivity. A complementary component uses Sentinel-1 and Dynamic World during 2015–2024 on separately constructed historical water cohorts. Finally, a stratified sample in four selected months compares JRC binary labels with dated Sentinel-2 proxies and assisted human interpretation. These components differ in period, spatial support and outcome; their trend estimates are not pooled.

### 2.3 Fixed footprints, observation effort and annual outcomes

The retrospective reference footprint contains pixels classified as seasonal or permanent water in at least one year during 2000–2024. The annual comparison footprint is its subset with a valid annual class in every one of those 25 years. Although primary outcomes stop in 2021, footprint construction therefore uses subsequent observation availability and water classification. This restriction is intentional and retrospective; it does not represent an independently defined wetland inventory or an operational forecasting domain. Within each region and year, seasonal and permanent areas are sums of pixel areas assigned the corresponding annual source class on that fixed footprint.

A separate transition diagnostic compared strict five-year majority classes in 2000–2004 and 2017–2021 on the same annual footprint. All five annual observations were required in each window, and at least three occurrences defined the majority class. A window without a strict majority remained ambiguous. These transitions describe source-class assignments and do not validate land-use or storage change.

Observation-effort summaries include the area-weighted number of observed calendar months, the number observed during June–September, and the area fraction observed in all twelve months. These diagnostics distinguish annual class availability from a fully observed annual water cycle. Permanent source classification is not treated as independent confirmation that water was visible in every month. For the original monthly JRC analysis, candidate months required coverage thresholds of 80%, 90% or 95% on both the district raster domain and the retrospective footprint. The common monthly cohort intersects valid pixels across all candidate months. A retained outcome required complete observation on that cohort, at least 48 candidate months and at least 20% footprint retention. All support failures remain in Supplementary Data.

Initial seasonal and permanent cohorts required all five annual observations in 2000–2004 and at least three occurrences of the respective class. Ambiguous and initially nonwater pixels were excluded from these two cohorts. Original monthly JRC inference for initial-class cohorts began in 2005, while the all-water series began in 2000. A monthly detection on an initial permanent cohort does not relabel that cohort as a new permanent class each month. The all-water common footprint retained 2.46%, 17.36% and 50.35% of its reference area under the 80%, 90% and 95% candidate-month gates, respectively. These rules select different restricted cohorts; their differences are not a coverage-treatment effect.

### 2.5 Radar–optical analysis and support-sensitive agreement

The 2015–2024 radar–optical analysis reconstructed its own retrospective annual ever-water footprint on a fixed approximately 30 m angular grid. Two additional frozen cohorts required all five initial annual classes to be observed and a strict majority of seasonal or permanent source classes during 2000–2004. These historical cohorts retained their initial labels throughout the radar analysis. They are distinct from the annual comparison footprint.

Sentinel-1 GRD scenes were restricted to interferometric wide swath, VV/VH availability, incidence angles of 30–45° and one pass/relative-orbit track. A deterministic metadata-based ranking selected descending relative orbit 48 before water-result comparisons. Selection considered a footprint proxy from at most the first six scenes, then temporal scene availability, scene count and lexical ties; it did not measure valid pixel coverage. Actual support was evaluated subsequently. Input scenes were placed on a common EPSG:32645 10 m working grid. Scenes on the same acquisition date were mosaicked, and a monthly pixel required at least two unique acquisition dates. Median VV, already in decibels, was classified at −19, −17 and −15 dB. These are strict, central and loose benchmark specifications without local independent calibration; no additional speckle filter was applied.

For track ranking only, the metadata footprint calculation simplified the boundary by 1,000 m and quantized the footprint proxy to 0.001; actual water summaries retained the original boundary. Dynamic World land evidence used trees, grass, crops, shrub_and_scrub, built, bare and snow_and_ice. Component-wise temporal medians of water and maximum-land scores were not assumed to form one probability distribution summing to one. The mean paired-footprint fraction includes all 120 months, including zero-support months, whereas pooled agreement divides summed agreeing area-time by summed paired area-time.

For Dynamic World, daily mosaics supplied monthly median water probability and median maximum probability among the seven selected land classes. Flooded vegetation was excluded from the land maximum. At least two unique dates were required. At each threshold , water evidence required water probability at least ; nonwater evidence required land probability at least  and water probability at most . Other pixels remained unclassified. These model scores were not treated as calibrated local probabilities of correctness. Dynamic World shares Sentinel-2 optical lineage with the dated interpretation component, so the optical comparisons are dependent (Brown et al., 2022).

Overlap-weighted supported and water fractions were aggregated from the working grid to the fixed coarse grid. A coarse comparison cell required at least 95% supported footprint and a water fraction of at least 80% or at most 20% of its supported area. Agreement was the summed agreeing area-time divided by summed paired area-time; retained paired footprint was reported alongside it. Monthly composites may represent different acquisition dates between sensors, adding genuine within-month change to classification and registration differences. No confidence threshold was selected by maximizing agreement.

### 2.6 Drought definition and recovery identifiability

SPI-3 and SPI-6 were calculated from complete-calendar rolling CHIRPS rainfall totals, using a fixed 1981–2020 calibration. Calendar-month-specific gamma maximum likelihood fixed location at zero and estimated zero probability separately, followed by a normal-quantile transformation. Calibration required at least 30 usable accumulations and ten positive accumulations for each calendar month. Failed fits remained missing. This is a precipitation-only drought index, without a validated precipitation–evapotranspiration index.

An SPI-3 event began at ≤−1 and ended at the first subsequent month with SPI ≥0; missing climate data censored active events. For radar recovery, eligible monthly envelopes required at least 95% supported area. Calendar-month reference envelopes were estimated from eligible 2015–2021 observations, with at least five reference observations. Subtracting the upper reference from the current lower endpoint, and the lower reference from the current upper endpoint, gave a conservative anomaly envelope. The pre-event reference required three consecutive observed months. A detectable drought deficit had to exceed 0.02 of cohort area; recovery required a return within 0.02 of the pre-event anomaly for two consecutive observed months, with up to 24 months of follow-up.

For numerical stability, cumulative probabilities were bounded away from 0 and 1 at 10−8. In the recovery calculation, the drought deficit excludes the first non-drought termination month. All event records retain their original missing-support status. Neither repeated dates within a sample nor overlapping event–cohort records are independent replicates.

Possible and confirmed recovery times were sought separately from the anomaly endpoints. Missing pre-event, drought-phase or follow-up evidence prevented a duration estimate. No interpolation bridged gaps. Sensitivity crossed coverage gates of 80%, 90% and 95% with minimum calendar-month reference counts of three, four and five. The resulting event–cohort records overlap across districts, union and cohorts. They are accounting records, not independent drought replicates. Original JRC recovery diagnostics were retained separately because their calendar reference and observation domains differ.

### 2.7 Dated sample comparison and assisted interpretation

The frozen comparison frame comprised March 2018, November 2020, March 2023 and November 2024. Within each district, month and observed JRC binary class, 30 pixels were sampled without replacement, giving 24 strata and 720 pixel-months. Previously exposed review cells were excluded before this new sample was drawn. The design therefore applies to this eligible four-month population, rather than every pixel or year in the annual study. The frozen sampling seed and stratum populations are retained in the source records.

Actual Sentinel-2 Level-2A crop values and product XML calibration supported an initial spectral triage. The rules used modified normalized difference water index (Xu, 2006), normalized green–near-infrared difference, vegetation index and reflectance screens. At least 95% of the target footprint had to be clear and strongly classified on at least two dates spanning fourteen days. A boundary diagnostic flagged opposite spectral evidence in a 10 m surrounding ring. These deliberately restrictive heuristic rules were not trained or locally validated as an accuracy model. Although displays used a 10 m grid, the shortwave-infrared band and scene-classification layer retained their coarser native information content.

### 2.8 Exploratory robustness and landscape context

A revision protocol was frozen before the new sensitivity outputs were examined; the original study results were already known. Seven overlapping cohorts were retained: C1, the original 2000–2024 ever-water footprint with all 25 annual classes valid; C2, the same ever-water condition with validity required only through 2021; C3, ever-water and complete annual validity during 2000–2021; C4, any seasonal/permanent class in 2000–2004, all five baseline classes valid, and complete annual validity through 2021; C5, the same baseline-water condition with available observations each year; C6, 2000–2021 ever-water with at least twenty valid annual classes; and C7, all annually valid pixels through 2021 within the district boundaries, without an ever-water restriction. C7 is still an observed subset, not full-district coverage. C1–C7 union areas were 1,276.41, 1,276.41, 1,271.76, 1,196.72, 1,252.36, 1,472.73 and 1,299.60 km², respectively. Available-support analyses required at least 95% annual coverage; missing-area bounds were retained rather than classifying gaps as nonwater.

For each cohort, first-to-final one-, three- and five-year windows complemented whole-series trends. A multi-year comparison required every year in both windows to pass its support rule. A separate monthly diagnostic intersected valid observations for each calendar month across all 22 primary years within the baseline-water footprint; a trend required retention of at least 20% of that footprint. This diagnostic measures the cost of equal calendar-month support and cannot reconstruct missing seasons.

The annual model sequence included time alone; time plus June–September rainfall; those terms plus annual observed months, wet-season observed months and the 2016 indicator; an additional preceding October–May rainfall term; and the original annual-climate control specification in Equation (1). Each class was modeled separately. Fits required at least five residual degrees of freedom. Calendar-distance HAC lags zero and two, all leave-one-year-out fits, and a short 2013–2021 time/monsoon analysis were retained. Approximate fixed-design circular residual block bootstrap intervals used lengths two, three and four, 2,000 replicates and seed 20261019. Bootstrap intervals were unavailable for nonconsecutive selected years; gaps were not compressed. District BY families comprised six district-by-class tests per cohort/model. These overlapping sensitivities are not independent confirmations.

New chronological checks used two-year tests starting in 2013, 2017 and 2020, with at least twelve earlier training observations. Mean and trend-only benchmarks were fitted on the same accepted training/test folds as each candidate. Pooled RMSE was the square root of total test squared error divided by test count. A constant processing indicator invalidated earlier training designs, leaving only two folds/four test years for controlled models, compared with three folds/six years for the monsoon model. These retrospective conditional hindcasts neither validate water classification nor establish operational forecasting performance.

HydroRIVERS Asia version 1 centerlines supplied static river context (Lehner and Grill, 2013), and ESA WorldCover 2020 version 100 supplied a static 10 m cropland class (Zanaga et al., 2021). River buffers were formed in EPSG:32645 at 200, 500 and 1,000 m; 500 m was the designated central setting. WorldCover classes were transferred by modal aggregation to the native JRC grid. Within each cohort, river proximity and, away from rivers, 2020 cropland versus other cover formed exhaustive groups. Buffer ranges are sensitivity ranges, not confidence intervals. This context does not identify individual ponds, historical cultivation, irrigation abstraction or river discharge.

A missingness-aware synthetic diagnostic placed rectangular deficits of zero, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10 and 0.20 of cohort area at every monthly onset during 2015–2024, for durations of one, three and six months. A zero anomaly baseline returned abruptly at the pulse end, so the imposed recovery time was zero months. The diagnostic retained actual observation gaps, anomaly-envelope half-widths and calendar-reference eligibility, requiring three pre-event observations and two recovery confirmations. It tested detectability under those widths, not a physical drought model or empirical recovery distribution. No actual missing water observations or reference labels were filled.

### 2.9 Reproducibility and analytical scope

The original computational audit verified 9,393 source-file checksums and reconciled export inventories. The revision additionally rechecked all fifty annual/monthly source rasters, reconstructed the original annual summaries to within 10⁻⁶ km², and reran the explicitly frozen exploratory models. The corrected dated-label version and its history were unchanged. Protocols, source versions, output tables, failed fits and SHA-256 manifests accompany the code. An initial calendar-gap bootstrap implementation was corrected before the reported outputs were frozen; superseded intervals are excluded. The environment used Python 3.12, NumPy 2.3.5, pandas 2.3.3, statsmodels 0.14.5 and rasterio 1.5.0. Computational checks do not establish environmental accuracy or confirmatory inference.

## 3 Results

### 3.1 Seasonal source-class decline on a restricted footprint

The fixed annual footprint was 1,276.41 km², retaining 79.30% of the retrospective ever-water reference area. District contributions were 473.82 km² in Naogaon, 358.88 km² in Chapainawabganj and 443.71 km² in Rajshahi. These areas define the estimand for the primary annual results; they do not cover the complete districts.

Between 2000 and 2021, union seasonal source-class area decreased from 938.53 to 699.91 km², a loss of 238.62 km² or 25.42% relative to the initial seasonal class area (Figure 2; Table 2). The largest district endpoint loss was in Naogaon: 145.96 km², or 37.84%. Rajshahi lost 73.38 km², or 24.46%, and Chapainawabganj lost 19.28 km², or 7.62%. The unadjusted union seasonal linear slope was −8.42 km² year−1 (95% interval −12.79 to −4.05; 22 annual observations). Endpoint differences and the fitted slope summarize different aspects of the trajectory.

Permanent source-class area increased from 65.49 to 92.37 km² across the same endpoints, a gain of 26.88 km² or 41.04%. Most of that endpoint increase occurred in Rajshahi and Chapainawabganj; Naogaon’s permanent-class area changed by only 0.14 km² (Table 2). These descriptive gains do not establish continuous permanent-water expansion after accounting for observation and processing changes.

Among pixels with a majority in both windows, 264.1 km² moved from seasonal to nonwater and 110.1 km² from nonwater to seasonal; seasonal-to-permanent and permanent-to-seasonal transitions were 29.2 and 32.8 km². ### 3.2 Observation and processing controls change the permanent-water interpretation

Annual class availability concealed substantial variation in within-year observation effort. On the union footprint during 2000–2021, the area-weighted observed-month count ranged from 5.30 to 10.25 months per year. June–September effort ranged from 0.17 to 2.87 of four months. The footprint fraction observed in every calendar month ranged from 0% to 16.15% (Figure 2). Thus, the annual dataset did not provide a fully observed twelve-month water cycle over most of the retained area.

Seasonal time coefficients remained negative in the union across the observation-control specifications (Figure 4). With climate, annual and wet-season observation effort, and the 2016 indicator included, the union coefficient was −0.0607 logit share year−1 (95% interval −0.0814 to −0.0399). District coefficients were −0.1154 in Naogaon (−0.1602 to −0.0706; adjusted p=0.00109), −0.0480 in Rajshahi (−0.0714 to −0.0245; adjusted p=0.00454) and −0.0350 in Chapainawabganj (−0.0720 to 0.0020; adjusted p=0.302). The district distinction is material: the final specification supported a continuing seasonal decline in two districts, while uncertainty included zero in the third.

### 3.3 Higher automated agreement coincides with lower paired support

The radar–optical analysis reconciled 1,440 monthly region/cohort rows and 5,760 threshold-specific rows. Its scene inventory contained 265 Sentinel-1 scenes and 1,274 Dynamic World scenes, totaling 1,539 unique source identifiers. These complete exports did not imply complete pixel observations in every month.

For the union historical ever-water cohort, increasing the Dynamic World threshold from 0.5 to 0.7 reduced the number of months with any classified support from 76 to 74 of 120. Mean classified support fell from 17.94% to 4.45% of the fixed cohort. Conditional Sentinel-1–Dynamic World agreement increased from 89.04% to 98.71%, while mean paired support decreased from 11.07% to 3.29% (Figure 6). At 0.6, agreement was 95.94% on mean paired support of 5.88%. Threshold 0.8 yielded no classified or paired support. Its missing agreement value indicates absence of comparison evidence.

After preserving date conflicts and the single documented correction, 194 reviewed points had consistent binary dates, 35 showed temporal change and 16 had uncertain dated evidence (Figure 9; Table 3).

The meteorological drought history is shown in Figure 10. SPI-3 describes rainfall deficits, so its return to zero does not itself establish surface-water recovery.

Under the primary radar recovery specification, none of the 108 event–cohort records yielded an identifiable duration. No minimum, maximum or scalar recovery time was available under these settings. Relaxing coverage and reference counts changed the reason for non-identification in some records, without producing reliable timing. Because records overlap and observational censoring can be informative, these counts do not estimate the proportion of independent droughts that failed to recover. The finding concerns the inability of the available sequence and method envelope to identify recovery; ecological recovery itself remains unresolved.

Synthetic controls exposed an additional limitation (Figure 12B,C). For the union historical-water cohort, only 13, four and zero of 120 possible starts retained the complete pre-event-to-confirmation sequence for one-, three- and six-month pulses. Across all 25,920 region/cohort/duration/deficit scenarios, no injected deficit was confirmed, including deficits of 0.20 of cohort area. Wide real anomaly envelopes prevented confirmation even where the calendar was continuous. Zero detections for the zero-deficit controls cannot demonstrate specificity when the positive controls also fail. These simulated results explain limited detectability under the declared method; they are not measured drought deficits, recovery times or evidence that real recovery did not occur.

## 4 Discussion

### 4.1 Seasonal decline is a monitoring signal with a defined spatial scope

The mapped seasonal decline is supported by more than the choice of two endpoint years, but its magnitude depends on the comparison. The original endpoint decrease was 25.42%, whereas five-year means gave 14.70% on the original footprint and 18.79% on the baseline-water complete cohort. Whole-series associations and these window comparisons support investigating seasonal source-class contraction within retained areas. They do not justify one district-wide loss percentage. District uncertainty also matters: the controlled seasonal models retained clearer evidence in Naogaon and Rajshahi than in Chapainawabganj.

Several mechanisms remain compatible with the observed seasonal-class loss. Water occurrence may have changed, pixels may have moved between seasonal and permanent classes, or the interaction of observation timing and processing may have affected annual assignment. The fixed footprint removes changes in which pixels enter each annual area sum, but it does not remove class error or uneven seasonal sampling. Neither a persistent time coefficient nor a climate-adjusted residual identifies irrigation abstraction, land conversion, river management or an ecological mechanism.

Static landscape context narrows where follow-up could begin. The larger proportional seasonal decrease away from mapped rivers in 2020 cropland was consistent across the tested buffers, while the distant noncropland group gained seasonal-class area. However, a 2020 land-cover label cannot describe cultivation throughout 2000–2021, and a global centerline buffer cannot separate all river water from ponds. River migration, wetland conversion, crop-water management and observation effects remain rival explanations. No local groundwater or discharge time series was acquired to discriminate among them.

Regional evidence of rainfall heterogeneity (Esha et al., 2026) and coupled climatic and groundwater stress (Sarker et al., 2026) makes those mechanisms relevant hypotheses. Testing them would require matched observations of groundwater levels, abstraction, river regulation, land use and waterbody management. Basin-scale separation of storage components uses different evidence, including process models and gravity observations (Prusevich et al., 2025). Our classified areas do not supply equivalent information about water volume. Likewise, the surface-water expansion reported in southwestern Bangladesh (Huda et al., 2021) is compatible with a different regional trajectory; neither result should be generalized across the country without harmonizing domains and definitions.

The permanent class illustrates the risk of interpreting endpoints as a robust process trend. Its 41.04% endpoint increase is a valid description of source classifications on the retained footprint. However, the annual time coefficients lost clear positive support after the 2016 indicator. The appropriate interpretation retains both findings. A continuous increase in physically permanent water, an increase in storage, and improved dry-season water security are not established by this record.

### 4.2 Agreement should be evaluated together with the area it represents

Raising the Dynamic World threshold removed much of the classification and paired footprint while increasing agreement. This is consistent with selecting clearer, more homogeneous cases, although the present analysis does not isolate the causes of selection. High agreement on that subset cannot describe the omitted mixed, cloudy or weak-probability areas. Threshold 0.8 is particularly instructive: an apparently demanding confidence rule produced no comparison support for this implementation and domain. Treating its missing results as dry land would reverse their meaning.

The assisted sample has a related support limitation. The hybrid comparison expanded assigned support beyond the highly selected automatic subset, while some interpretations remained unresolved. Reporting 76.54% assigned area-time alongside 98.56% conditional agreement makes the coverage limitation visible. The binary-completion range additionally shows the influence of unresolved weight without assuming random missingness or inventing labels. It still depends on the assigned proxy labels and cannot account for their possible errors. Accuracy-assessment literature requires reference quality and sample design to be evaluated separately (Olofsson et al., 2014; Stehman and Foody, 2019). Our assisted route meets a different purpose: documenting available evidence and unresolved support.

Cross-sensor studies with external reference imagery (Vanderhoof et al., 2023) and monitoring studies incorporating field observations (Slinski et al., 2025) provide stronger designs for future validation. Their reported performance cannot be transferred to the thresholds or wetland settings used here. The Bangladesh flood-insurance comparison similarly concerns a different outcome, but its finding that data choice affects decision performance (Saunders et al., 2025) supports the practical value of retaining divergence and support information. For district monitoring, agreement should accompany explicit maps or summaries of the population actually compared.

Independent reference evidence remains a separate requirement. A published dataset of expert-delineated, 3 m PlanetScope surface water provides a relevant design example (Mukherjee et al., 2024), but its availability does not establish eligible reference coverage in these districts and months. The attempted provider download returned an access-block page rather than label data; no such files were used. Dates, spatial overlap, class definitions and independence would need verification before applying external labels. The assisted interpretations already collected here were retained honestly rather than relabeled as independent truth.

### 4.3 Climate association and drought resilience require different evidence

Climate adjustment addressed a limited associational question. The monsoon model improved on its matched mean benchmark but not its trend benchmark; reduced observation/processing models improved on both on only four test years. Those results neither establish robust forecasting skill nor identify a climatic or management cause. Approximate HAC and residual-block intervals, leave-one-year-out fits and simpler specifications expose sensitivity; they cannot compensate for a short series or an imperfect observation process.

Recovery timing requires more than an annual association or several well-interpreted dates. The pre-event baseline, full drought phase and successive recovery observations must be connected on an interpretable calendar. Here, gaps and uncertain deficits prevented that chain under every examined setting. Hydrological drought can persist beyond a rainfall deficit because of storage and seasonal processes (Van Loon, 2015), but this analysis cannot quantify that persistence. It also cannot rank the districts by recovery speed.

The negative identifiability result is useful for monitoring design. Additional observations should be targeted to missing parts of an event sequence, especially pre-event conditions and successive months around climatic drought termination. Reprocessing more isolated clear images may improve an annual map without resolving those event-level gaps. A future recovery study would benefit from a predefined network of sentinel waterbodies with repeated dated imagery and water-level observations, coupled to a stable reference method. Such a network is a proposed next study, not an intervention evaluated here.

### 4.4 Implications for sustainable water management

For the target districts, the results support a staged use of satellite evidence. Seasonal-class losses on the retained footprint can identify areas for field or higher-resolution investigation. Before using those areas to prioritize storage restoration or water allocation, managers would need to determine whether the signal reflects persistent inundation change, land use, channel movement or classification behavior. The present district aggregates do not locate a validated restoration site or quantify a water-supply benefit.

A practical district dashboard could report four quantities together: source-class area, observation effort, the fraction of the intended footprint with a usable comparison, and the continuity of evidence around drought events. This would preserve the distinction between a complete download, a classified pixel and a continuous hydrological record. The results give empirical reasons for each item: restricted annual coverage, sparse wet-season observations, threshold-dependent paired support and unidentified recovery duration.

This approach is relevant to monitoring the extent of water-related ecosystems and to climate-adaptation planning. It is not a complete official assessment of Sustainable Development Goal indicator 6.6.1, which has its own ecosystem definitions, reporting dimensions and national implementation requirements. Water quality, groundwater storage, biodiversity outcomes, household water access and the effects of management interventions were not measured. The management contribution is therefore better-specified evidence for investigation and monitoring, without claiming observed improvements in water security.

### 4.5 Limitations and transferability

Retrospective selection is the main spatial limitation. The ever-water footprint uses the full 2000–2024 annual record, and the annual common footprint also requires future-year validity. Transient monthly-only water and poorly observed pixels can be excluded. Equal inclusion of retained pixels across years improves comparability within that subset, but it does not make the subset representative of the entire districts. Raster-center district assignment in the local annual analysis also differs from the radar analysis’s geometry-weighted area accounting.

The baseline-water cohorts reduce dependence on future water occurrence, and the 2021-validity alternatives remove the requirement for later annual availability. They do not remove annual-class error, nonrandom missingness or the need for within-year observations. C7 also covers only 1,299.60 km² of annually valid pixels, far below the complete reporting boundaries. The fact that only November passed the equal-calendar retention gate prevents interpreting the monthly check as a fully controlled annual hydroperiod analysis.

Source classification and alignment remain important. Corrected annual products, Landsat collection differences, mixed boundary pixels, SAR threshold behavior, wet soil, rice and vegetation can all affect water estimates. The method envelopes cover specified thresholds and unknown support, while excluding some systematic errors. Dynamic World and the dated optical proxy have related inputs, and monthly sensor composites do not necessarily represent the same day. Independent annual seasonal/permanent accuracy remains unmeasured.

Statistical uncertainty is also incomplete. Twenty-two annual observations provide limited information for models with multiple climate, effort and processing terms. HAC intervals are approximate and pointwise. Reported adjustment families cover specified district tests, not the entire exploratory workflow; spatial dependence and unobserved drivers remain. Retrospective cohorts make the temporal checks conditional hindcasts. The four-month proxy bootstrap addresses sampled pixel-month variation, without resolving nonrandom reference failure or spatial clustering. The lack of independent labels is explicit and prevents presenting proxy agreement as accuracy.

These limitations bound transferability to similar monitoring problems. Estimates should be reported together with their spatial, temporal and reference support. The numerical trends and thresholds themselves remain specific to the declared products, domains and periods in northwestern Bangladesh.

## 5 Conclusion

Mapped seasonal JRC source-class area decreased within the retained annual-observation footprint in northwestern Bangladesh. The 2000–2021 endpoint decline was 25.42%, compared with 14.70% between five-year means on the same footprint and 18.79% on a baseline-water complete cohort. Negative seasonal associations persisted across the tested specifications, with stronger controlled district evidence in Naogaon and Rajshahi. Permanent-class endpoint gains remained sensitive to observation and processing controls. These findings concern classified areas and declared spatial support, not district-wide water storage.

The complementary analyses identify limits that matter for water-management interpretation. Higher radar–optical agreement occurred on a smaller paired footprint; assisted dated interpretation left 23.46% of sampled design weight unresolved; and no drought-recovery duration was identifiable across nine support/reference specifications. Satellite-based climate-resilience assessment should therefore retain observation density, paired support and event continuity alongside mapped change. These results guide further investigation and monitoring design while leaving causal drivers, independent accuracy and ecological recovery unresolved.
